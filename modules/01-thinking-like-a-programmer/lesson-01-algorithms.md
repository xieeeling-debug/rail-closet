# 01.1 — Algorithms in Plain English

## You will learn

- What an algorithm is  
- How to write steps clearly  
- Sequence, decisions, repetition  

## Definition

An **algorithm** is a finite list of clear steps that solves a problem.

Cooking recipes are algorithms. So are subway directions. So is sorting your closet by color.

## Three building blocks

1. **Sequence** — do steps in order  
2. **Decision** — if this, do A; else do B  
3. **Repetition** — repeat until done  

### Example: Making tea

```text
1. Fill kettle with water
2. Turn kettle on
3. Wait until water boils
4. Place tea bag in cup
5. Pour water into cup
6. Wait 3 minutes
7. Remove tea bag
```

### Example: Choosing an outfit (closet)

```text
1. Ask: Is it raining?
2. If yes → pick waterproof shoes
3. Else → pick regular shoes
4. Pick a top from "Tops"
5. Pick a bottom from "Bottoms"
6. Show the pair
```

Notice: no Python yet. Clarity first.

## Bad vs good steps

**Bad:** “Organize the files somehow.”  
**Good:** “Create folders `images` and `docs`. Move `.png` files into `images`. Move `.pdf` into `docs`.”

Computers need **unambiguous** instructions. Humans writing for computers must practice that clarity.

## Practice

Write algorithms (8–12 steps each) for:

1. Brushing your teeth  
2. Finding the largest number in a list of 5 numbers written on paper  
3. Uploading a clothing photo into a closet app (imagine the clicks)
